## 0.1.4 — 2026-10-19

**Fix: on RER Managed, backup, restore and erasure refused. Penny 0.1.3 and every earlier version are affected.**

RER Managed (RER Platform) installs Penny with its own migration ledger, the `rer_migrations` table. Penny 0.1.3 and earlier did not recognise it, so on a Managed installation the Data page's Export this workspace, Restore a backup and Erase personal data (and their APIs and commands) all refused with `unclassified_archive_schema`: the owner could not back up, move or restore Penny. 0.1.4 treats `rer_migrations` as the installation's bookkeeping, by its exact name and using Base's list of platform tables. It never goes into a backup, and a restore never changes the destination's ledger. On every backup and restore the ledger must list exactly this version's migrations with their file hashes; anything else (an edited, missing, extra or half-applied migration) refuses with `migration_history_mismatch`. Every other unknown table, including other `rer_` names, still refuses. Local and other hosted installations are unaffected. Nothing to do beyond updating.

## 0.1.2 — 2026-10-17

**Security fix: erasing a person also deleted other people's data. Every Penny version up to and including 0.1.1 is affected. Upgrade now.**

On 0.1.1 and every earlier version, erasing a requester (Data → Erase personal data, `data:erase` or the erasure API) deleted more than that person's own records. Everything hung on their requests went with them:

- evidence an operator recorded from someone else and linked to the request, such as a sales call, with its versions, original files, links to candidate bets and the committed decision's evidence copies, so the decision lost that evidence;
- the requester's requests, with other customers' votes, comments and follows on them and the requests' roadmap and release links;
- followers' release snapshots and the release answers they gave.

Upgrading stops further loss; it does not bring back what an earlier erasure deleted. Those records can be recovered only from a backup made before that erasure. Penny refuses to restore that backup into the installation that ran the erasure, because it is older than the erasure. Restore it into a new, empty installation instead: a backup made by 0.1.0 or 0.1.1 restores directly into 0.1.2; an older one restores into a new installation of the version that made it, which you then upgrade to 0.1.2. An installation-level copy of the D1 database and R2 files made before the erasure works too. The backup also holds the erased person's data, so erase them again on 0.1.2 afterwards.

Update with `git pull`, `rer install` and `rer setup` (a hosted copy runs `rer deploy`). Migration 0062 adds the installation-only data runner tables; `rer setup` (or `rer deploy`) applies it and keeps every record. An erasure or restore left stopped by an earlier version shows Resume (and Cancel while nothing changed yet) on the Data page.

- Erasing a person removes only their own data. A request and the evidence it was submitted or imported as go together; any other evidence stays with its links (decisions keep their evidence count), and a requester's own requests stay for everyone else without their name or words.
