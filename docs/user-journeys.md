# Penny user journeys: set direction once, keep learning

**Proposed experience, not current capability or authority.** Penny's main branch is still a read-only D1-backed application. The journeys below describe the flagship destination in [the product plan](../FLAGSHIP-PLAN.md) and [frontier extension](frontier-extension.md). Every name, quote, count, prototype and outcome in this document is illustrative; a live workspace must use authorized customer evidence or show an honest empty state.

Penny is for the product lead who sets a direction and needs the work to keep moving. The owner chooses a learning goal when one is clear, grants the smallest useful source and action scope, and makes choices with real consequences. Penny prepares the rest: it brings evidence together, proposes a useful question when no goal exists, compares credible explanations, builds and tests real alternatives inside its grant, measures what happened, and returns only when a choice or authority boundary actually needs a person. Silence never expands access or effects. Work that remains inside a valid grant continues without another approval; blocked work pauses at the boundary while independent in-scope work continues.

## A small, quiet experience

The existing Penny home is the starting point; the proposed experience adds no research or agent-control dashboard. It has three quiet views:

- **Results** holds measured outcomes and material findings, including mixed, null, negative and incomplete results. A shipped change, completed analysis or passing test does not mean the customer problem was solved.
- **Learning** shows the current question, what materially changed, the next check and when Penny will make it. If nothing changed, Penny says so once and keeps working within its active grant.
- **Needs you** contains only a consequential choice, authority request, action-dependent identity question, consent issue or exception that Penny cannot safely resolve within its grant.

**Results** and **Learning** are informational, not a stream of tasks to review. A person can inspect a result, correct an interpretation, or pause work when useful. Only **Needs you** contains an actionable card: one reasoned recommendation and why it matters now; the strongest supporting and contradicting evidence; known uncertainty and what could change the view; a real preview of the evidence, prototype or proposed effect; the consequence, scope and cost; and one action with **Do this**, **Change it**, and **Not now** controls. The recommendation is advice, not an automatic bet ranking. Alternatives remain visible on common dimensions required by `DEC-003`, with no combined score. A person can open source spans, measurement details and technical evidence deliberately; ordinary use does not expose model choices, taxonomy setup, schemas or agent orchestration.

Source scope and execution authority are plain-English choices made when they matter: which source and records Penny may read for which goal, and which classes of work it may do (for example, analyze, create isolated branches and run tests, contact consenting participants, or change a named production cohort). Penny proposes a narrow useful scope and fills in known details. External contact, publication, access expansion, production changes, spending beyond a grant and a new strategic commitment are never hidden inside one global autopilot switch or granted by default. Routine work creates no push notification. The owner may opt into one weekly digest of material changes; otherwise Learning stays quiet. A consequential card creates one notice and can receive at most one reminder before its predeclared deadline or grant expiry. Silence does not approve it: at expiry Penny stops only the affected effect and leaves unrelated in-scope work running. Controls to pause one investigation or all work, inspect actions and cost, correct an interpretation, and disconnect a source stay easy to find in the existing workspace.

## 1. First value from one source

Mara opens Penny after a planning meeting and has no prepared research prompt. Penny offers two direct starts: **Use one available source** or **Open existing feedback**. It names the purpose and narrow read scope it proposes before access is granted. It does not require connecting every service, filling a blank prompt, designing labels or mapping a schema. If Mara has no goal, Penny proposes one from the first permitted record and lets her correct it inline.

She selects the product's existing support source for customer-learning use and confirms the proposed record scope. Penny imports only records the source permits and that every Penny operator may access under `OPS-001`. It prepares the source mapping, retains source IDs and replay keys, and preserves original wording, observed time, creator and any identity the source actually supplied. A missing person or account stays unknown; the model cannot fill it in. One item can produce a useful, cited observation such as, “This customer could not find the accountant export; the record does not show whether access also failed.” Penny marks that as a possibility, not a pattern or a verified customer fact beyond the cited item.

Mara's effort is choosing one authorized starting point and purpose, then correcting the proposed goal only if it is wrong. She does not clean imported fields or create a research taxonomy. The first result appears in **Learning** with the source excerpt and one next check. If the source is empty, too sparse or lacks a valid use basis, Penny says exactly that, shows no invented sample as customer evidence, and does not make a background read until access is granted. If Penny's own mapping fails, it uses the source schema and original record to repair and validate that mapping inside the grant; it asks Mara only if access, a source-side setting or a real use-policy choice is missing. Other permitted work can continue.

## 2. A customer contributes once, in the channel they already use

Illustratively, Amira tells the support team in its normal conversation that month-end close stalls when her accountant cannot tell which export is current. She does not receive a second survey or have to copy the story into a Penny portal. If the source and record are authorized for this product-learning purpose, its connector brings the original conversation and event ID into the one investigation. Penny keeps Amira's identity only if the source supplied it, leaves the account unset if it did not, and avoids contacting her again through a duplicate channel.

Penny sees that one clarification could distinguish “the accountant cannot access the file” from “the accountant cannot identify the current version.” It checks the purpose-specific consent and channel policy first. Where standing outreach authority and Amira's consent both cover this exact purpose and source channel, Penny sends one concise optional question in that same thread and captures the response through the authorized connection. Mara does not need to approve that routine send again. If either authority or consent is absent, Penny sends nothing; it asks Mara once only if outreach is consequential and a single grant decision would unblock it, otherwise it continues with existing evidence. Support participation, a vote, an entered email address and a release subscription do not by themselves grant research contact. A reply to Penny's no-reply email cannot create a Penny record (`NOTIFY-004`).

Amira's effort is sharing her problem once, and answering one useful question only if it is offered through a permitted channel. No extra identity verification is imposed unless a specific action needs an identity the source does not provide. If she ignores or declines the optional question, Penny records nonresponse or refusal without treating it as a negative answer and does not ask her again through another channel. If Penny cannot use the source for all equal operators, it excludes the record and explains the access boundary to Mara instead of importing restricted data.

## 3. Discover and prototype a new workflow

Across authorized support examples and already permitted product events, Penny notices a pattern that existing labels miss: customers resend exports, ask which version the accountant used, and check whether corrections were included. Its working hypothesis is a broader job—finish a shared monthly close without handoff confusion—not simply “add accountant access.” Penny preserves the separate examples and their uncertainty, checks counterexamples and affected segments, and looks for evidence that would distinguish a new close workflow from a clearer export.

Mara has already granted Penny permission to inspect a named product repository, create isolated branches, use a test tenant and spend up to a stated research/build ceiling. That grant excludes deployment, production data and customer contact beyond separately consented research. Penny now continues without asking Mara to approve each internal step. It reproduces the real close task, builds competing working alternatives in isolated branches—a clearer CSV handoff, a read-only close packet, and a shared correction workflow—and checks them against the same permission, revision and recovery requirements. These are prototypes in the owner's actual code, not mock application screens. Penny also keeps no-build as a real alternative.

The eventual **Needs you** card gives Mara a runnable preview, task evidence, failed checks, source excerpts, counter-evidence, scope and actual spend. Its illustrative copy could read: “**Recommendation:** Try the shared close packet with five already-consenting teams for two weeks, up to $400 in study costs. Several conversations mention version confusion, while established teams still finish with CSV; this trial distinguishes a shared handoff problem from a clearer export. We still do not know whether teams will finish by the third business day without staff help. [Run this test] [Change the plan] [Not now].” The sample audience, duration and cost are fictional, not performance claims or consent. Where the grant allows a test with already-consenting participants, Penny runs it and reports their task results separately from synthetic test findings. It does not infer demand from simulated users. Alternatives remain comparable rather than automatically ranked; Penny explains why this is its recommended next action and what it does not yet know. Mara's effort is one strategic review after the scoped investigation is complete, not a series of technical approvals. If an input, environment or effect falls outside the grant, Penny stops at that boundary and prepares one card naming the exact missing authority. Until then, silence lets the permitted internal work finish.

## 4. Repair a known failure from reproduction through measured delivery

An existing authorized product event identifies a known failure: an accountant invitation is rejected during month-end close. Mara's earlier plain-language direction was “help customers finish this close handoff without staff intervention”; her separate grant permits isolated changes and a small, expiring rollout to a capped, opted-in group, with a spend limit and rollback if access worsens. Penny prepares the eligible group, baseline, comparison, observation window and stop check from the goal, available source records and that grant. Mara does not fill in a measurement form. This is a complete repair run with **zero new owner decisions**.

Penny first reproduces the denied invitation against the actual product in the test tenant and preserves the failing steps. It builds and tests two real repairs: a clearer permission explanation at the invite step and a corrected role assignment flow. The shared task and security checks show which repairs pass; Penny applies the already-approved selection rule and does not invent a new ranking. It prepares numerator, denominator, baseline and comparison from the known goal, then deploys the selected change only to the grant's bounded cohort. The tracker remains authoritative; Penny records its real delivery receipt, spend and rollback state. The team is not asked to approve a branch, test run, measurement query or other routine step.

Penny watches the predeclared window and measures whether customers complete the close task, not merely whether invite errors fall. It stops and rolls back if the stated condition is reached. If the existing grant did not cover a required production or contact effect, Penny would pause at that boundary and show one complete approval card with audience, cost, duration, stop rule and rollback. It would send at most one reminder before the expiry; silence withholds the effect and stops only that work at expiry. All independent in-scope research continues. During a covered run, however, the absence of a new owner reply does not interrupt progress or result capture.

## 5. Record a negative result and follow the standing rule

Suppose the bounded repair reduces invite denials but does not improve month-end completion. Penny reports both observations in **Results** with the actual cohort, numerator, denominator, window, comparison, missing follow-ups and adverse measures. Its summary says, “The change reduced invite failures; this test has not shown that month-end close improved.” It does not convert release into success, call a null result “resolved,” or suppress a finding because the result is disappointing.

Before the pilot, Mara and Penny's agreed protocol already state what happens next: continue measurement once if the sample and remaining budget allow; otherwise stop at the declared boundary, preserve the result as inconclusive or negative, and note the evidence that would change the view. Penny applies that rule without asking Mara to invent a next step after every null. It keeps useful result and no-build paths available. The **Results** view gives the actual comparison and a deliberate **Inspect result** link, not a new approval task. A new strategic investment, wider cohort or unapproved contact would need one decision card, but routine recording and authorized follow-through do not. If standing authority and participant consent permit a useful follow-up, Penny sends the agreed optional in-app or source-channel question once and keeps refusal, nonresponse and each answer distinct. A correction or later finding is a new version linked to the original commitment snapshot, not a rewrite of the past.

The customer contributes no additional effort unless a consented follow-up can answer a decision-relevant question. The owner sees one honest result, not a pile of measurement setup tasks. If a denominator or source is missing, Penny labels the result incomplete and follows the predeclared stop/research rule. It does not quietly extend the test, widen the population or spend more.

## 6. Correct, pause, revoke and recover

Mara is away for two weeks. Routine imports, task checks and in-scope work produce no push messages. She may opt into one weekly digest of material changes. If a consequential choice appears, Penny posts one **Needs you** card and one notice; at most one reminder arrives before the recorded deadline or grant expiry. If Mara does not answer, Penny waits. On grant expiry it stops only the affected read, contact or production effect; allowed internal work continues, and no permission is inferred. The card remains visible with the exact effect held and the one action that would resume it. If a source grant itself expires, future reads pause at that point.

On return, Penny gives one prepared account: **Completed:** the bounded invitation repair reduced denials but did not improve month-end completion; improvement remains unproven. **Still learning:** the permitted source and test-tenant comparison is continuing; Penny's next check is Friday, to see which close-handoff steps still fail in the shared-packet versus clearer-export task. **Held:** the proposed two-week trial with five already-consenting teams, capped at $400, has not started. Mara can inspect its existing card, audience, measures and stop rule, then choose **Run this test**, **Change the plan**, or **Not now**. Silence neither starts the trial nor expands permission.

When Mara returns, she can pause just that investigation, pause all Penny work, inspect its action and cost history, correct an interpretation inline, or disconnect the source. She does not need to inspect logs or repair a queue. If a mapping or derived index is broken, Penny repairs and reconciles it from original source IDs where possible. If a choice still requires Mara and she is unavailable, Penny continues other valid work and holds only that effect; it does not reassign authority, escalate through a hidden role, or ask other operators repeatedly. Operators retain the equal access in `OPS-001`.

If Mara corrects “the export was missing” to “the export existed but was the wrong month,” Penny versions the interpretation, keeps the source wording, identifies affected decisions and updates future recommendations. The original decision and outcome snapshots remain intact. If she revokes the connector, Penny stops future reads, checkpoints queued work, identifies dependent derived records and follows the selected retention or erasure policy. Reconnecting resumes only after Penny rechecks scope, consent and erasure state; provider IDs and effect receipts prevent duplicate imports, messages or deployments. Erasure takes precedence over history under `DATA-003` and the baseline. Silence never revives an expired grant, and a paused external effect is never replayed just because a workflow resumed.

Mara's effort is one correction or one reconnect choice when needed. Penny carries it through indexes and affected work, shows the remaining consequence, and leaves source-specific technical detail behind a deliberate inspection. If recovery is incomplete, it says what remains paused and why; it does not claim a successful resume.

These journeys are product hypotheses. The [acceptance plan](flagship-acceptance.md) must measure setup, review, customer effort, interruptions, correction and recovery as part of the total work—not treat agent completion or a passing test as a customer outcome.
